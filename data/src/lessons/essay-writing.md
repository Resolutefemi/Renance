## What the examiner rewards

Every WAEC essay is marked for content, organisation, expression and
mechanical accuracy. Content means you answered the exact question asked;
organisation means a clear plan a stranger can follow; expression means
varied, precise sentences; accuracy means grammar, spelling and punctuation
that do not interrupt the reader.

- Underline the key instruction: narrate, argue, describe, or explain
- Narration wants events in time order; argument wants points plus rebuttal
- A formal letter earns its salutation, indentation and complimentary close

> Key point: half of lost marks are lost to answering a question the paper did not ask.

## The five-paragraph plan

The safest structure for a 450-word essay is one you can rehearse tonight.

1. Introduction: restate the topic in your own words and announce your stance
2. First body paragraph: your strongest point, one example, one explanation
3. Second body paragraph: next point, developed the same way
4. Third body paragraph: acknowledge the other side, then answer it
5. Conclusion: restate the stance and close with one memorable line

Each paragraph is a **point, example, explanation** sandwich. One idea per
paragraph; the moment you start a new idea, start a new paragraph.

### The paragraph rule

A paragraph opens with a topic sentence, develops it with two or three
supporting sentences, and closes by linking to the next paragraph. If you
cannot name the topic sentence, the paragraph has no job.

## The errors that cost the most

- Tense drift: pick past or present for narration and hold it to the end
- Subject-verb disagreement: "the boys were", not "the boys was"
- Ambiguous pronouns: every "he", "she" and "it" must point at one clear noun
- Spelling of small words: "receive", "accommodation", "government", "alright is not accepted, use all right"
- Punctuation: a comma splice joins two full sentences with only a comma

> Key point: reserve five minutes at the end to hunt these five errors. It is the cheapest mark you will ever buy.

## Practice loop

Write one timed essay per week on paper, mark it against the four criteria,
and rewrite only the weakest paragraph. Rewriting beats re-reading: the
hand learns the fix the eye merely noticed.
